Welcome, Young Musician!

Today we are going to learn about intervals.

Think about this: When you walk from one place to another, there is a distance between the two places. Music also has distance!

Let us discover how far apart two musical notes are.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain what an interval is.
  • Tell when two notes are close or far apart.
  • Count simple note distances.
  • Give simple examples such as Do–Re and Do–Mi.
  • Recognise simple intervals on a music staff.

📖 What Is an Interval?

An interval is the distance between two musical notes.

Let us use our feet! 👣

If you take one small step, the distance is small. If you take several steps, the distance is bigger.

12345

In music, we also look at how far one note is from another note.

🌟 Real-Life Examples

🚶

Walking

One step is close. Several steps cover a greater distance.

🪜

Stairs

Moving from one stair to another shows distance between positions.

🦘

Jumping

A bigger jump covers more distance than a small step.

🎹

Music

Do–Re is closer than Do–Sol when we count the note names.

🎼 Intervals on the Music Staff

Music notes can be written on the lines and spaces of a staff. The position of one note compared with another helps us see their distance.

Remember: An interval tells us the distance between two notes. The notes may be close together or farther apart.

✏️ Classwork

  1. What is an interval in music?
  2. Is Do–Re closer or farther than Do–Sol?
  3. How many note names are counted in Do–Mi?
  4. Give one real-life example of distance.
  5. Complete: Do → Re → ___ → Fa.

2. Do–Re is closer.
3. Three.
4. Walking, stairs, or jumping.
5. Mi.

Remember These

An interval is the distance between two musical notes.

We can understand intervals by thinking about walking steps, stairs and jumps.
